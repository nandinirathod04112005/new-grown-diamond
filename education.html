Chapter 01 · The Origin Question

Natural vs lab-grown

A lab-grown diamond is a diamond — pure crystallised carbon with the same hardness, fire and brilliance as a mined stone. The only difference is where its story begins.

Natural

Formed by the Earth

Crystallised over one to three billion years, deep beneath the surface under immense heat and pressure — then carried upwards by ancient volcanic activity and recovered by mining.

Lab-Grown

Grown by science

Crystallised in a matter of weeks inside a controlled reactor, where carbon builds on a diamond seed — the same atoms in the same structure, traceable from its very first day.

Chapter 02 · Growth Methods

CVD vs HPHT

Two routes to the same crystal. Both grow real diamonds — the name simply describes the machine that grew them.

CVD

Chemical Vapour Deposition

A thin diamond seed is placed in a vacuum chamber filled with carbon-rich gas. Energised into a plasma, the gas releases carbon atoms that settle onto the seed layer by layer until a rough diamond stands complete.

  • Grows at moderate pressure and around 800 °C
  • Favoured for high-clarity, larger stones
  • The method behind our own reactors
HPHT

High Pressure, High Temperature

The original growth method recreates the conditions of the deep Earth: a carbon source and seed are pressed to over 50,000 atmospheres at roughly 1,500 °C until the carbon crystallises into diamond around the seed.

  • Mimics how natural diamonds form underground
  • Often used for growing seeds and refining colour
  • Produces equally real, certifiable diamonds
◆

Both methods grow real diamonds. CVD and HPHT stones are chemically, physically and optically identical to mined diamonds. Laboratories grade them to exactly the same standards — and the certificate always states which method grew your stone.

Chapter 03 · The Grading Language

The four Cs

Cut, colour, clarity and carat — four measurements that together describe every diamond on Earth. Learn them once and every certificate makes sense.

Cut

How well the facets are angled and finished. Cut is what makes a diamond sparkle — better proportions return more light to your eye, so prioritise it above everything else.

Colour

How close a white diamond is to truly colourless. Grades run from D down the alphabet as a faint warm tint appears — D to F are colourless, while G and H still look white to the eye.

Graded D → Z

Clarity

Nearly every diamond carries tiny internal birthmarks called inclusions. Clarity measures how visible they are under 10× magnification — VS2 and above usually looks clean to the eye.

Graded FL → I3

Carat

A diamond’s weight, not its size — one carat equals 0.2 grams. Two stones of equal carat can look different depending on shape and cut, so always judge carat alongside cut.

Chapter 05 · Certification

Why certification matters

A certificate is a diamond’s passport — an independent laboratory’s verdict on exactly what you are buying, issued before the stone is ever priced.

  • ◆ Confirms the 4Cs were graded by an independent gemmologist — not by the seller.
  • ◆ Registers the stone’s identity and growth method in the laboratory’s database.
  • ◆ Protects insurance, resale and upgrade value for the life of the diamond.

Chapter 06 · The Paperwork

How to read a diamond report

Every laboratory report follows the same logic. Find these eight lines and you can read any certificate in under a minute.

  1. Report number — the certificate’s unique ID, usually laser-inscribed on the diamond’s girdle as well.
  2. Shape & measurements — the outline plus dimensions in millimetres (length × width × depth).
  3. Carat weight — measured to the hundredth of a carat.
  4. Colour grade — the stone’s position on the D–Z colourless scale.
  5. Clarity grade — FL to I3, judged under 10× magnification.
  6. Cut, polish & symmetry — Excellent to Poor; aim for Excellent or Very Good across all three.
  7. Fluorescence — the stone’s reaction to UV light, None to Strong; largely a matter of taste.
  8. Growth method — lab-grown reports state CVD or HPHT plus any post-growth treatment.
◆

Quick authenticity check: match the report number on the certificate to the laser inscription on the girdle, then look the number up in the laboratory’s free online database.

Chapter 07 · Aftercare

Caring for your jewellery

Diamond is the hardest material on Earth — but settings, metals and neighbouring gems still appreciate a little ritual.

✦

Clean gently

Warm water, a drop of mild soap and a soft brush — then rinse and pat dry with a lint-free cloth.

◇

Store separately

Diamonds scratch other jewellery — and each other. Give every piece its own pouch or lined compartment.

✿

Wear thoughtfully

Jewellery goes on last — after lotions and perfume — and comes off for the gym, garden and pool.

✓

Service yearly

Have prongs, clasps and settings checked once a year to keep every stone exactly where it belongs.
