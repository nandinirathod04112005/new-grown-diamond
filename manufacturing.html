Manufacturing

From growth
to brilliance.

Nine stages carry a single carbon seed to a certified stone and a finished piece of jewellery — every one of them under our roof.

Follow the journey
The Process

Nine stages, one standard

01
01

CVD Diamond Growth

A diamond seed enters the reactor. Carbon plasma settles on it atom by atom for weeks until a gem-grade crystal stands complete.

02
02

Rough Diamond

The chamber opens to raw, unrepeatable carbon — every rough logged, weighed and traced from its first day.

03
03

Planning

Scanners map the rough in three dimensions. Master planners chart the cut that keeps the most light — and the most carat.

04
04

Laser Cutting

A cold laser parts the rough along the planned lines with sub-millimetre precision, facet by future facet.

05
05

Polishing

Each facet meets the spinning scaife until it sits at its exact angle — fifty-seven chances to bend light perfectly.

06
06

Quality Inspection

Gemmologists check colour, clarity, cut and symmetry under 10× magnification before a stone may leave the atelier.

07
07

Certification

Independent laboratories — IGI and GIA — grade every stone and issue the report that travels with it for life.

08
08

Finished Diamond

Certified, catalogued and photographed: the finished stone joins the inventory, ready to be chosen.

09
09

Jewellery Creation

In the atelier, goldsmiths set the stone by hand into its final piece — the end of one journey, the start of another.

Quality Control

Checked at every turn

A stone can fail our inspection at any stage — and go back to the wheel until it cannot.

10×

Loupe-clean checks

Every facet reviewed under 10× magnification.

4C

Graded in-house first

Colour, clarity, cut and carat measured before the lab ever sees it.

◆

Batch traceability

Each stone tracked from reactor to report number.

✓

Master gemmologists

Final sign-off rests with people, not machines.

Certification

Independently graded

We grade every stone ourselves — then hand it to the world's leading laboratories to grade again. Only when both agree does a stone earn its place in our inventory.
